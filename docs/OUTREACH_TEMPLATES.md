## Cold Email Templates

### Template A: Cost/Supply Focus (For Tier A nurseries)

**Subject Lines (A/B test):**
- "[First Name], bulk growing media question for [Company]"
- "Wisconsin supplier for [Company]'s spring season"
- "Growing media supply for [production volume] operation"

**Body:**

```
Hi [First Name],

I noticed [Company] runs [greenhouse/container/specific operation from research] in [City]. 

We supply bulk growing media to Wisconsin nurseries like [similar operation]. Most see:
- 15-25% cost savings vs. Pro-Mix/SunGro retail pricing
- Consistent quality across seasonal orders
- Same-day/next-day delivery in [Region]

Worth a quick call to see if our pricing works for [Company]'s [spring/summer] season?

Best,
[Your Name]
[Company]
[Phone] | [Email]
```

**Customization:**
- For organic-certified nurseries: Add "OMRI-certified organic blends available"
- For large operations (>10K sq ft greenhouse): Mention "custom blend formulations"
- For Wisconsin-based: Emphasize "Wisconsin supplier - no cross-country freight"

## Phone Script (Cold Call - Tier A Contacts)

### Opening

```
Hi [First Name], this is [Your Name] with [Company] here in Wisconsin.

Quick question - are you the right person to talk to about potting mix and growing media supply, or should I speak with someone else?

[If yes: continue. If no: "Who handles that?" Get transfer or name]

Great! I'll keep this brief. We supply bulk growing media to nurseries around [Region]. The reason I'm calling is that a lot of growers in [County/Region] mention wanting:
1. Better pricing than the national brands
2. Faster delivery (we're in-state)
3. Custom blends for specific crops

Is that something worth exploring for [Company], or are you all set with your current supplier?

[Listen - proceed based on response]
```

### Discovery Questions

**If they express interest:**

1. "What volume do you typically order per season?" (Qualify: need >10 cu yd minimum)
2. "What are you currently using?" (Identify competitor/brand)
3. "Any pain points with your current supplier - delivery timing, consistency, cost?"
4. "Are you doing any organic production?" (OMRI opportunity)
5. "When do you typically place your bulk orders?" (Timing/seasonality)

### Qualification Criteria

**Good Fit Signals:**
- 10+ cu yd annual volume
- Container/greenhouse production
- Currently buying Pro-Mix, SunGro, or similar
- Price sensitivity mentioned
- Organic certification or interest
- Decision-maker on the phone

**Poor Fit Signals:**
- <5 cu yd volume (too small)
- Retail-only operation (not wholesale grower)
- Just bought full season supply
- No authority/interest

### Closing

```
Based on what you've shared, I think our pricing would make sense for [Company].

Next step: I can send you a price sheet and get a sample pallet delivered - usually within 3-5 days. That way you can test quality before committing.

Does that work, or would you prefer [alternative]?

[Get commitment, send calendar invite or follow-up task]

Great! You'll have pricing by [day] and we can coordinate sample delivery.

Thanks for your time, [First Name]!
```

## Objection Handling

### "We're happy with [Pro-Mix/SunGro/Current Supplier]"

**Response:**
"That's great - [Brand] makes good products. Out of curiosity, what do you like most about them? [Listen] 

The reason I ask is that most of our customers were using [Brand] before switching. They typically mention the price difference (15-25% savings) or delivery speed as reasons they tried us. 

Would it be worth getting a sample pallet just to compare quality? No cost, no obligation."

**Goal:** Plant seed of doubt, low-risk trial

---

### "We don't have budget to switch suppliers"

**Response:**
"I totally understand. The good news is that switching to us usually *reduces* your growing media costs, not increases them. Most nurseries save $X-Y per cubic yard vs. retail pricing.

Would it make sense to just model out the savings for your typical volume? If the numbers don't work, I'll tell you straight up."

**Goal:** Reframe as cost *reduction*, not expense

---

### "We buy locally/support local suppliers"

**Response:**
"We're Wisconsin-based too - [Location]. All our blending happens in-state, which is why we can do same-day or next-day delivery to [Region].

A lot of the 'national' brands you might be using actually ship from out-of-state and just have a distributor here. We're local supply *and* local manufacturing."

**Goal:** Claim local supplier status

---

### "I need to talk to [owner/manager/purchasing]"

**Response:**
"Absolutely - would it help if I joined that conversation to answer any technical questions about blends or pricing? Or would you prefer I send materials you can forward along?

Also happy to just send a sample pallet to [Name] directly if that's easier."

**Goal:** Get to decision-maker or provide tools for internal champion

---

### "Can you send me information first?"

**Response:**
"Of course - I can email over:
- Current price sheet
- Blend options (standard, organic, custom)
- Delivery zones and timing
- Sample request form

And then maybe we circle back in a week once you've had a chance to review?

What's the best email?"

**Goal:** Send materials, set follow-up, stay in control

---

### "We're all set for this season already"

**Response:**
"Makes sense - most nurseries lock in their supply ahead of time. When do you typically start planning for [next season] - [Month/Quarter]?

I'll reach back out then. In the meantime, want me to send over our price sheet just so you have it on file?"

**Goal:** Set future follow-up, stay top-of-mind

## Seasonal Messaging Calendar

### Q1 (Jan-Mar): Spring Season Prep

**Hook:** "Spring production planning - lock in supply now"

**Pain Points:**
- Running out of inventory mid-season
- Delivery delays during busy season
- Price increases if ordering last-minute

**CTA:** "Reserve spring inventory" / "Schedule delivery windows"

---

### Q2 (Apr-Jun): Peak Season

**Hook:** "Mid-season restocking" / "Unexpected growth"

**Pain Points:**
- Need quick turnaround on additional volume
- Can't wait weeks for national supplier delivery
- Quality consistency across multiple orders

**CTA:** "Same-week delivery available" / "Emergency inventory"

---

### Q3 (Jul-Sep): Fall Planning

**Hook:** "Fall crop prep" / "Overwintering supply"

**Pain Points:**
- Prepping for fall mums, pansies, ornamental cabbage
- Planning for next spring (early bird pricing)
- Storage space for bulk orders

**CTA:** "Fall delivery schedule" / "Early 2027 pricing"

---

### Q4 (Oct-Dec): Next Year Planning

**Hook:** "2027 planning" / "Lock in pricing"

**Pain Points:**
- Budget planning for next year
- Avoiding price increases
- Securing delivery windows during peak season

**CTA:** "2027 pricing commitment" / "Reserve spring delivery slots"

## Template Usage Notes

**DO:**
- Personalize first line with specific research
- Use contact's name (not "Hi there")
- Mention specific crops/focus if known
- Keep emails <100 words
- Have ONE clear call-to-action

**DON'T:**
- Send generic mass emails
- Use all-caps or excessive exclamation marks
- Make claims you can't back up (pricing savings, delivery speed)
- Oversell - let them ask questions
- Follow up more than 3 times without response
